      Recent 5K times, by a distance. A runner’s most recent 5K form (best 5K or 5000m in 2025–26, or since 2023 if they haven’t raced one lately) explains 84% of the variation in where they finished. Best-ever handicap comes next (62%). Current handicap explains less than half, largely because Power of 10 reset handicaps in June 2026 (see the note on handicap below). Power of 10’s national ladder isn’t shown separately: it is a ranking of the same handicap, so it adds nothing new.

      The women stuck closer to form than the men: 5K form explained 84% of their finishing order against 74% in the men's race. The order of predictors was the same in the men's race.

      R² is the square of the rank correlation between each predictor and finishing place, among runners who have that figure. 100% would mean everyone finished exactly in predicted order. One race each, so treat it as a first look.

      How it works. Only runners with a ranking are compared. They are put in order by ranking (faster 5K form, or lower road handicap, comes first) to give an expected place, and in order of today’s finish to give an actual place among the same group. “Vs form” is expected place minus actual place, so ▲20 means 20 places better than predicted. The figure after it (for example 1.6×) is how many times better or worse than predicted that is, which is what sets the colour.

      Colour bands use the ratio of expected to actual place, so moving 20 places matters more near the front than at the back. As expected: within about 20%. Ahead/behind: up to 1.7×. Well ahead/behind: more than 1.7× (and at least 5 places).

      The year after each 5K form time is when it was set, taken from the yearly bests on their Power of 10 profile. 5K form is each runner’s fastest 5K or 5000m in 2025–26. If they haven’t run one in that time, it uses their best since 2023, so runners who have slowed aren’t judged on old times. A 2025–26 time more than 10% slower than their current Power of 10 handicap suggests (say, a jogged parkrun) is treated as a one-off and their best since 2023 is used instead; these are marked *, and hovering shows why. Runners with no 5K since 2023 are left out of this view.

      Handicap is Power of 10’s road handicap: now, and the best it has been with the month it was reached. Like golf, lower is better. Runners on the same handicap are separated by their national ladder position, which Power of 10 ranks on the unrounded handicap, so it is more precise than the one decimal place shown. Power of 10 recalculated handicaps in mid-June 2026: in the space of a fortnight around a quarter of Surrey League runners moved 1.5 or more worse, with no change in their running. So current handicaps are less reliable than usual early this season, and a best handicap from before June 2026 isn’t directly comparable with one set since.

      Sources. Results from surreyleague.org, provisional on the day. Handicaps and best times from Power of 10, read 10–11 October 2026. Rows marked “check match” were found by name search and their Power of 10 club differs from today’s.
